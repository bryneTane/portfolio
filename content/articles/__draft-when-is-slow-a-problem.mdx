I build [Lumorem](https://lumorem.com), a tool that finds what makes apps slow and sorts it by how much it hurts the people using them. Sorting like that meant digging into the research on how humans experience waiting. What it says is simple, and it explains a lot of everyday life with a phone.

## Your brain has speed limits

A wait doesn't hurt in proportion to its length. It hurts when it crosses one of three lines that [research](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/response-times-3-important-limits/) keeps finding, decade after decade:

A [2025 study](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3706598.3713475) even measured people slowing down their own gestures after an app kept them waiting, as if bracing for the next delay. On a tool someone uses all day, a wait keeps costing after it's over.

## A wait shrinks when you can watch the work

Researchers ran an [experiment](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.1110.1376) with a travel search site: when the screen showed what was being searched ("checking airlines... comparing fares..."), people rated the site higher. Some preferred the slower version over an instant one, and trusted its results more.

## The point

The same three seconds can be invisible in one app and fatal in another. Milliseconds are easy to measure; whether they matter is a question about people. That question is the one Lumorem is built to answer, and the technical version, with the research behind each piece, is in [Building Lumorem's impact score](/articles/building-lumorem-impact-score).
